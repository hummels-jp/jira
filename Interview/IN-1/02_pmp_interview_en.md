# Common PMP Project Management Interview Questions & Answers (100 Questions)

## I. PMP Fundamentals and Project Management Framework

<a id="q1"></a>
1. **Q: Please explain what a project is, and the main differences between a project and day-to-day operations.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q1) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q1) | [英](#q1)

   **A:** A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result, with a defined beginning and end. Compared to operations, projects are one-time, temporary, and progressively elaborated, whereas operations are ongoing and repetitive. Both work together to support the achievement of organizational strategic objectives.

<a id="q2"></a>
2. **Q: What are the differences between a Project, a Program, and a Portfolio?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q2) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q2) | [英](#q2)

   **A:** A project focuses on a single deliverable; a program is a group of related projects, subsidiary programs, and program activities managed in a coordinated way to obtain benefits not available by managing them individually; a portfolio is a collection of projects, programs, and operational work managed together to achieve strategic objectives. The three levels progress hierarchically, with strategic significance increasing at each level.

<a id="q3"></a>
3. **Q: What is the PMBOK Guide? What core content does it contain?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q3) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q3) | [英](#q3)

   **A:** PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge) is the project management knowledge system guide published by PMI and is the core reference for PMP certification. The 7th Edition is centered around 12 principles and 8 performance domains, while the 6th Edition is organized around 5 process groups and 10 knowledge areas, covering best practices in project management.

<a id="q4"></a>
4. **Q: What are the five process groups of project management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q4) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q4) | [英](#q4)

   **A:** The five process groups are: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, and Closing. They span the entire project life cycle and can be applied iteratively in each phase, forming a PDCA-style continuous management cycle.

<a id="q5"></a>
5. **Q: What are the ten knowledge areas in PMBOK 6th Edition?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q5) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q5) | [英](#q5)

   **A:** They are Integration Management, Scope Management, Schedule Management, Cost Management, Quality Management, Resource Management, Communications Management, Risk Management, Procurement Management, and Stakeholder Management. Each knowledge area contains several specific processes, which intersect with the five process groups to form 49 project management processes.

<a id="q6"></a>
6. **Q: What is the project life cycle? What are the common types?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q6) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q6) | [英](#q6)

   **A:** The project life cycle is the series of phases a project passes through from initiation to closure. Common types include: predictive (waterfall), iterative, incremental, adaptive (agile), and hybrid. The choice depends on factors such as requirement clarity, change frequency, risk, and stakeholder involvement.

<a id="q7"></a>
7. **Q: What are the core responsibilities of a Project Manager (PM)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q7) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q7) | [英](#q7)

   **A:** The project manager is responsible for leading the team to achieve project objectives, including planning, executing, monitoring, and closing. Core responsibilities cover scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, resource, communication, and stakeholder management. The PM must also navigate organizational politics, change control, and team motivation, serving as the person accountable for business value delivery.

<a id="q8"></a>
8. **Q: What does the PMI Talent Triangle refer to?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q8) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q8) | [英](#q8)

   **A:** The PMI Talent Triangle represents the three core competency areas required for PMP recertification: Technical Project Management (Ways of Working), Leadership (Power Skills), and Strategic and Business Management (Business Acumen). Modern project managers need not only methods and tools but also soft skills and business insight to drive strategic value.

<a id="q9"></a>
9. **Q: What is the difference between a Project Charter and a Project Management Plan (PMP)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q9) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q9) | [英](#q9)

   **A:** The Project Charter is an output of the initiating phase, signed by the sponsor, formally authorizing the project and empowering the project manager, containing high-level objectives and business justification. The Project Management Plan is produced in the planning phase and describes in detail how the project will be executed, monitored, and closed—it is the concrete blueprint for execution.

<a id="q10"></a>
10. **Q: What is Organizational Project Management (OPM)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q10) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q10) | [英](#q10)

   **A:** Organizational Project Management is an execution framework that converts organizational strategy into business outcomes through the coordinated management of projects, programs, and portfolios. It emphasizes alignment among project management, strategy, organizational culture, and resource allocation, helping organizations achieve sustainable competitive advantage.

## II. Project Initiation and Business Case

<a id="q11"></a>
11. **Q: What are the main objectives of the project initiation phase?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q11) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q11) | [英](#q11)

   **A:** The goal of the initiating phase is to formally define and authorize a new project or phase, clarifying its purpose, objectives, high-level requirements, and key stakeholders. The core outputs are the project charter and stakeholder register, laying the foundation for subsequent planning and ensuring the project aligns with organizational strategy.

<a id="q12"></a>
12. **Q: What is typically included in a project charter?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q12) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q12) | [英](#q12)

   **A:** The project charter typically includes the project purpose, measurable objectives and success criteria, high-level requirements, assumptions and constraints, high-level risks, milestones, budget summary, stakeholder list, approval requirements, and the project manager's name and authority. It is the "birth certificate" of the project.

<a id="q13"></a>
13. **Q: What is a Business Case? Why is it important?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q13) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q13) | [英](#q13)

   **A:** The Business Case is a document that justifies the investment in the project, including needs analysis, cost-benefit analysis, alternatives, and ROI. It is the basis for project authorization, helps senior leadership decide whether to invest in the project, and serves as the baseline for continuously assessing value during execution.

<a id="q14"></a>
14. **Q: How do you identify project stakeholders?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q14) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q14) | [英](#q14)

   **A:** Techniques include brainstorming, analyzing the organizational chart, expert judgment, interviewing existing stakeholders, and reviewing historical project archives. Identification should begin as early as possible and cover all individuals or organizations that may affect or be affected by the project, with results recorded in the stakeholder register.

<a id="q15"></a>
15. **Q: What is the stakeholder register? What information does it contain?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q15) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q15) | [英](#q15)

   **A:** The stakeholder register records information about all identified stakeholders, including name, role, contact information, needs and expectations, influence/interest, attitude (supportive/neutral/resistant), and classification. It is the foundational document for stakeholder management and must be continuously updated throughout the project.

<a id="q16"></a>
16. **Q: What role does the Project Sponsor play in a project?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q16) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q16) | [英](#q16)

   **A:** The sponsor provides funding and resources for the project, signs the project charter to formally authorize the project, and is the project's top-level supporter. The sponsor is responsible for removing organizational obstacles, making major decisions, coordinating senior stakeholders, and approving change requests. The sponsor is a key ally for the PM when facing issues that cannot be resolved at lower levels.

<a id="q17"></a>
17. **Q: What are SMART goals? Please give an example.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q17) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q17) | [英](#q17)

   **A:** SMART stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Example: "By Q2 2026, increase customer satisfaction score from 80 to 90"—this is a goal set according to SMART principles.

<a id="q18"></a>
18. **Q: What are common financial evaluation methods used for project selection?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q18) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q18) | [英](#q18)

   **A:** Common methods include Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), Payback Period, Return on Investment (ROI), and Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR). Generally, higher NPV is better, higher IRR is better, and shorter payback period is better. Strategic fit should also be considered when making a selection.

<a id="q19"></a>
19. **Q: What is a Feasibility Study?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q19) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q19) | [英](#q19)

   **A:** A feasibility study is an analysis performed before formal project authorization to evaluate whether a project is worth undertaking. It covers technical, economic, legal, operational, and schedule feasibility. It helps identify key risks and constraints, provides a basis for the authorization decision, and helps avoid investing in projects likely to fail.

<a id="q20"></a>
20. **Q: How do you manage stakeholder expectations effectively during project initiation?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q20) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q20) | [英](#q20)

   **A:** Understand each party's expectations through one-on-one interviews as early as possible; clearly document and manage differences in requirements; establish shared success criteria and communication cadence; promptly manage unrealistic expectations to prevent scope creep later; and ensure key stakeholders sign the charter to reach a common commitment.

## III. Project Scope Management

<a id="q21"></a>
21. **Q: What is project scope? What two aspects does it include?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q21) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q21) | [英](#q21)

   **A:** Project scope includes product scope and project scope. Product scope refers to the features and functions of the product, service, or result to be delivered; project scope refers to the work required to deliver those results. Scope management ensures the project does—and only does—the work that is needed, avoiding omissions and scope creep.

<a id="q22"></a>
22. **Q: What is a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)? Why is it important?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q22) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q22) | [英](#q22)

   **A:** A WBS (Work Breakdown Structure) is a hierarchical, structured method of decomposing project deliverables into smaller, more manageable work packages. It is the foundation for scope, schedule, cost estimation, and risk identification, ensuring no work is missed or duplicated, following the 100% rule.

<a id="q23"></a>
23. **Q: What is a Work Package?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q23) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q23) | [英](#q23)

   **A:** A work package is the lowest level element of the WBS—the smallest unit of work for which cost and duration can be estimated and a responsible person can be assigned. Work packages should conform to the "8/80 rule," typically requiring 8 to 80 hours to complete, making them easy to manage and track, and serving as an input to activity definition.

<a id="q24"></a>
24. **Q: What is Scope Creep? How can it be prevented?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q24) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q24) | [英](#q24)

   **A:** Scope creep refers to uncontrolled expansion of scope, usually caused by requirement changes, new stakeholder demands, or hidden expectations. Preventive measures include: thorough requirements gathering, clear baselines, a formal change control process (CCB), better communication, and promptly assessing change impact and obtaining authorization.

<a id="q25"></a>
25. **Q: How do you collect project requirements? What techniques are commonly used?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q25) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q25) | [英](#q25)

   **A:** Common techniques include: interviews, focus groups, facilitated workshops (e.g., JAD), questionnaires and surveys, observation, prototyping, benchmarking, brainstorming, affinity diagrams, mind mapping, and user stories. The choice depends on stakeholder count, requirement maturity, and project type.

<a id="q26"></a>
26. **Q: What is a Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q26) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q26) | [英](#q26)

   **A:** The Requirements Traceability Matrix links each requirement to its source, business objective, WBS, design, test cases, and acceptance criteria. It ensures every requirement is implemented and tested without omission, facilitates change impact analysis, and is an important tool for scope and quality management.

<a id="q27"></a>
27. **Q: What is the difference between Validate Scope and Control Quality?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q27) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q27) | [英](#q27)

   **A:** Control Quality focuses on the correctness of deliverables (whether they meet quality requirements) and is performed by the quality department; Validate Scope focuses on acceptance of deliverables (whether the customer accepts them) and is performed by the customer or sponsor. Typically Control Quality is performed before Validate Scope.

<a id="q28"></a>
28. **Q: What is the Scope Baseline?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q28) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q28) | [英](#q28)

   **A:** The Scope Baseline consists of three components: the Scope Statement, the WBS, and the WBS Dictionary. It is the approved version of the scope, which can only be modified through the formal change control process. The scope baseline serves as the basis for measuring project performance and assessing change impacts.

<a id="q29"></a>
29. **Q: How does scope management differ between predictive and agile projects?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q29) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q29) | [英](#q29)

   **A:** Predictive projects lock in detailed scope early and manage changes through change control; agile projects use rolling-wave planning, with the product backlog continuously evolving and incremental functionality delivered through each iteration, welcoming change. Agile emphasizes value-based prioritization rather than fixed scope.

<a id="q30"></a>
30. **Q: How would you handle a customer's ad-hoc addition of new requirements?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q30) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q30) | [英](#q30)

   **A:** First, record the requirement and thank the customer for their feedback; assess its impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, and risk; communicate the assessment with the sponsor and relevant parties; decide through the formal change control process whether to accept, reject, or defer; if approved, update the baseline and related plans and notify the team.

## IV. Schedule and Time Management

<a id="q31"></a>
31. **Q: What are the main processes of project schedule management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q31) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q31) | [英](#q31)

   **A:** The main processes include: Plan Schedule Management, Define Activities, Sequence Activities, Estimate Activity Durations, Develop Schedule, and Control Schedule. The core goal is to deliver project outcomes on time, ensuring schedule objectives are met through baseline management, variance analysis, and corrective actions.

<a id="q32"></a>
32. **Q: What is the Critical Path Method (CPM)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q32) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q32) | [英](#q32)

   **A:** The Critical Path Method uses a network diagram to identify the longest path of activities in a project—the critical path. Activities on the critical path have zero total float, and any delay will cause the project to be delayed. CPM is used to determine the shortest schedule and identify activities that require focused control.

<a id="q33"></a>
33. **Q: What are Total Float and Free Float?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q33) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q33) | [英](#q33)

   **A:** Total float is the maximum amount of time an activity can be delayed without affecting the project completion date; free float is the amount of time an activity can be delayed without affecting the early start of any successor activity. Activities on the critical path typically have total float equal to zero.

<a id="q34"></a>
34. **Q: What activity duration estimation techniques are commonly used?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q34) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q34) | [英](#q34)

   **A:** Common techniques include: analogous estimating (based on historical projects, quick but low accuracy), parametric estimating (based on statistical models), three-point estimating (PERT, considering optimistic/pessimistic/most likely), bottom-up estimating (highest accuracy but time-consuming), and data analysis. The three-point formula is (O+4M+P)/6.

<a id="q35"></a>
35. **Q: What is a Gantt Chart? What are its pros and cons?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q35) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q35) | [英](#q35)

   **A:** A Gantt chart is a schedule visualization tool that uses horizontal bars to represent activity durations. Pros: intuitive, easy to understand, good for communication. Cons: difficult to display complex dependencies and the critical path, and becomes hard to grasp holistically when activities are numerous. Typically used together with network diagrams.

<a id="q36"></a>
36. **Q: Please explain the four types of activity dependencies (FS, SS, FF, SF).**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q36) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q36) | [英](#q36)

   **A:** FS (Finish-to-Start): the predecessor must finish before the successor starts—the most common; SS (Start-to-Start): both activities start at the same time; FF (Finish-to-Finish): both activities finish at the same time; SF (Start-to-Finish): the successor cannot finish until the predecessor starts—rarely used.

<a id="q37"></a>
37. **Q: What are Resource Leveling and Resource Smoothing?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q37) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q37) | [英](#q37)

   **A:** Resource leveling adjusts activity start/finish dates based on resource constraints and may extend the project duration; resource smoothing adjusts activities within available float, keeping the critical path and duration unchanged. Both are resource optimization techniques—the former is "harder," the latter is "softer."

<a id="q38"></a>
38. **Q: What are Crashing and Fast Tracking?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q38) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q38) | [英](#q38)

   **A:** Crashing shortens the schedule by adding resources, typically increasing cost; fast tracking shortens the schedule by performing originally sequential activities in parallel, typically increasing risk and rework. Both are schedule compression techniques—choose based on trade-offs between cost, risk, and duration.

<a id="q39"></a>
39. **Q: What measures would you take when a project is falling behind schedule?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q39) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q39) | [英](#q39)

   **A:** First, analyze the cause and scope of the delay; identify activities on the critical path for special attention; consider options such as crashing, fast tracking, scope reduction, adding resources, and outsourcing; evaluate each option for cost, risk, and feasibility; communicate with the sponsor and stakeholders and execute once approved.

<a id="q40"></a>
40. **Q: What is the difference between a Milestone and an Activity?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q40) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q40) | [英](#q40)

   **A:** An activity is specific work that consumes time and resources and has a duration; a milestone is an important point in time or event in the project, with zero duration, used to mark the completion of a phase or a key decision point. Milestones are often used for progress reporting to management and stage sign-off.

## V. Cost and Budget Management

<a id="q41"></a>
41. **Q: What are the main processes of project cost management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q41) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q41) | [英](#q41)

   **A:** The main processes include: Plan Cost Management, Estimate Costs, Determine Budget, and Control Costs. The goal is to complete the project within the approved budget, ensuring return on investment and financial health through accurate cost estimation, reasonable budget allocation, and effective variance analysis.

<a id="q42"></a>
42. **Q: What are common cost estimation methods?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q42) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q42) | [英](#q42)

   **A:** Similar to schedule estimation, they include: analogous estimating (quick and rough), parametric estimating (based on historical data and statistics), bottom-up estimating (decomposed to work packages, most accurate), three-point estimating (accounting for uncertainty), and expert judgment. Analogous methods are common early in a project; bottom-up methods are used later.

<a id="q43"></a>
43. **Q: What is Reserve Analysis? What is the difference between Contingency Reserve and Management Reserve?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q43) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q43) | [英](#q43)

   **A:** Contingency Reserve is used to address identified risks, controlled by the PM, and is part of the cost baseline; Management Reserve is used for unidentified risks, controlled by management, not in the cost baseline but within the total budget. Both are funds set aside to address uncertainty.

<a id="q44"></a>
44. **Q: What is Earned Value Management (EVM)? What are its three core elements?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q44) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q44) | [英](#q44)

   **A:** Earned Value Management integrates scope, schedule, and cost to measure performance. The three core elements are: PV (Planned Value), EV (Earned Value / value of work completed), and AC (Actual Cost). EVM enables early detection of variances and forecasting of final outcomes.

<a id="q45"></a>
45. **Q: Please explain the meaning and interpretation of SPI and CPI.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q45) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q45) | [英](#q45)

   **A:** SPI (Schedule Performance Index) = EV/PV; >1 means ahead of schedule, <1 means behind. CPI (Cost Performance Index) = EV/AC; >1 means under budget, <1 means over budget. Together they reflect the health of the project and are core control metrics.

<a id="q46"></a>
46. **Q: How is the Estimate at Completion (EAC) calculated?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q46) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q46) | [英](#q46)

   **A:** EAC has several formulas: if remaining work will be done at the budgeted rate: EAC = AC + (BAC − EV); if the current CPI trend continues: EAC = BAC / CPI; considering both CPI and SPI: EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) / (CPI × SPI). The choice depends on expected performance for the remaining work.

<a id="q47"></a>
47. **Q: What are Variance at Completion (VAC) and To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q47) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q47) | [英](#q47)

   **A:** VAC = BAC − EAC, reflecting the final cost variance of the project; TCPI = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) or (BAC − EV) / (EAC − AC), representing the cost efficiency the remaining work must achieve. A TCPI > 1 indicates the remaining work must be performed more efficiently than planned, increasing pressure.

<a id="q48"></a>
48. **Q: What is the difference between the Cost Baseline and the Project Budget?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q48) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q48) | [英](#q48)

   **A:** Cost Baseline = total of all work package costs + Contingency Reserve; it is the approved baseline for measuring cost performance. Project Budget = Cost Baseline + Management Reserve; it is the total authorized funding for the project. The cost baseline is used for day-to-day control, while management reserve requires senior approval to use.

<a id="q49"></a>
49. **Q: What would you do when a project experiences cost overruns?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q49) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q49) | [英](#q49)

   **A:** Use EVM to analyze causes of the overrun (scope, productivity, risks, etc.); assess remaining work and EAC; identify controllable measures such as optimizing resources, cutting scope, avoiding rework, and negotiating with suppliers; escalate to the sponsor to evaluate whether to draw on management reserve or adjust the baseline; prevent further deterioration.

<a id="q50"></a>
50. **Q: What is Life Cycle Cost?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q50) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q50) | [英](#q50)

   **A:** Life Cycle Cost is the total cost of a product or system from concept, design, development, and operations through disposal, including initial investment and ongoing operations and maintenance costs. Life cycle cost—not just project cost—should be considered when evaluating projects, to avoid suboptimal "low upfront, high operational" decisions.

## VI. Quality Management

<a id="q51"></a>
51. **Q: What are the three core processes of project quality management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q51) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q51) | [英](#q51)

   **A:** Plan Quality Management (determine quality requirements and standards), Manage Quality (QA, put the quality plan into action and audit processes), Control Quality (QC, inspect deliverables against standards). These correspond to a "plan–assure–control" chain that ensures project outputs meet requirements.

<a id="q52"></a>
52. **Q: What is the difference between Quality Assurance (QA) and Quality Control (QC)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q52) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q52) | [英](#q52)

   **A:** QA is process-oriented, preventing defects through auditing and process improvement; QC is product-oriented, detecting defects through inspection. QA belongs to the executing process group, while QC belongs to the monitoring and controlling process group. The former focuses on "doing the right things," the latter on "doing things right."

<a id="q53"></a>
53. **Q: What is Cost of Quality (COQ)? What categories does it include?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q53) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q53) | [英](#q53)

   **A:** COQ includes cost of conformance (prevention cost + appraisal cost) and cost of nonconformance (internal failure + external failure). The more invested in prevention, the lower the failure cost. The COQ concept encourages investing upfront rather than fixing later; the optimal COQ lies at the balance point of prevention and failure costs.

<a id="q54"></a>
54. **Q: Please list several commonly used quality management tools.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q54) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q54) | [英](#q54)

   **A:** The seven basic quality tools include: cause-and-effect diagram (fishbone/Ishikawa), flowchart, checklist, Pareto chart, histogram, control chart, and scatter diagram. In addition, there are 5 Whys, affinity diagrams, force field analysis, etc. They are used to identify problems, analyze root causes, and monitor processes.

<a id="q55"></a>
55. **Q: What is the Pareto Principle? How is it applied in project quality management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q55) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q55) | [英](#q55)

   **A:** The Pareto Principle, or 80/20 rule, states that 80% of problems come from 20% of causes. In quality management, the Pareto chart is used to identify the few key causes of most defects, concentrating resources on those "vital few" to achieve the greatest improvement.

<a id="q56"></a>
56. **Q: What is a Control Chart? How do you determine if a process is out of control?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q56) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q56) | [英](#q56)

   **A:** A control chart is used to monitor process stability and includes a centerline and upper/lower control limits. Rules for judging out-of-control conditions include: points beyond the control limits, seven consecutive points on the same side (Rule of 7), obvious trends, or non-random patterns. When out of control, investigate special causes and take corrective action.

<a id="q57"></a>
57. **Q: What is Six Sigma?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q57) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q57) | [英](#q57)

   **A:** Six Sigma is a data-driven quality improvement methodology that aims to reduce process defects to 3.4 per million opportunities (3.4 DPMO). It uses the DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) methodology and is widely applied in manufacturing and services to improve quality.

<a id="q58"></a>
58. **Q: What is Deming's PDCA cycle?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q58) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q58) | [英](#q58)

   **A:** The PDCA cycle stands for Plan-Do-Check-Act and is the foundational methodology for continuous improvement. Both ISO and agile management draw on this idea. Through continuous cycles, processes and outcomes are gradually optimized to achieve continuous improvement.

<a id="q59"></a>
59. **Q: How do you ensure quality requirements are met in a project?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q59) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q59) | [英](#q59)

   **A:** Define quality standards and acceptance criteria and incorporate them into requirements; develop a quality management plan; perform QA activities such as peer reviews, code reviews, and testing; conduct QC inspections on key deliverables; use metrics to monitor quality trends; take timely corrective action on variances and pursue continuous improvement.

<a id="q60"></a>
60. **Q: What is "Gold Plating"? Why should it be avoided?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q60) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q60) | [英](#q60)

   **A:** Gold plating refers to the team providing additional features beyond requirements on its own initiative. Although well-intentioned, it increases cost, risk, and scope creep, may introduce defects, and may not even be wanted by the customer. Deliverables should strictly follow the scope baseline; any additional feature must be formally approved through the change control process.

## VII. Resource and Team Management

<a id="q61"></a>
61. **Q: What are the five stages of Tuckman's team development model?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q61) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q61) | [英](#q61)

   **A:** Forming (members get to know each other), Storming (conflicts arise), Norming (norms are established), Performing (efficient operation), and Adjourning (team disbands after the project). The PM should adjust their leadership style based on the stage.

<a id="q62"></a>
62. **Q: Please list several common methods of conflict resolution.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q62) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q62) | [英](#q62)

   **A:** Five methods: Avoiding (withdraw), Smoothing (accommodating), Compromising, Forcing (directing), and Collaborating (problem-solving). Collaborating is the best approach, producing win-win results; forcing and avoiding are short-term solutions.

<a id="q63"></a>
63. **Q: Please describe a time you handled conflict on a project team.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q63) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q63) | [英](#q63)

   **A:** On a cross-departmental project, the development and testing teams argued over responsibility for defects. I met one-on-one with both sides to understand their positions, then held a face-to-face meeting focused on facts rather than emotions, guiding both parties to co-create a defect classification and responsibility process. A written consensus was reached, and the conflict became an opportunity for process improvement.

<a id="q64"></a>
64. **Q: How can Maslow's hierarchy of needs be applied to team motivation?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q64) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q64) | [英](#q64)

   **A:** Maslow classified needs into five levels: physiological, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualization. The PM can motivate members based on their level: new employees value safety (stable contracts), senior employees value esteem (recognition), and high performers seek self-actualization (challenging tasks and growth opportunities).

<a id="q65"></a>
65. **Q: What is Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q65) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q65) | [英](#q65)

   **A:** Herzberg categorized factors affecting job satisfaction into hygiene factors (salary, working conditions, etc.—their absence causes dissatisfaction, but their presence does not motivate) and motivators (achievement, recognition, growth, etc.—which truly drive engagement). PMs should provide motivators while ensuring hygiene factors are met.

<a id="q66"></a>
66. **Q: What is a RACI matrix? How is it used?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q66) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q66) | [英](#q66)

   **A:** RACI is a role assignment matrix: R (Responsible—executes the work), A (Accountable—ultimately answerable), C (Consulted—provides input), I (Informed—kept updated). Each task must have exactly one A to avoid ambiguous accountability. It is an important tool for project governance and communications planning.

<a id="q67"></a>
67. **Q: What is a virtual team? What challenges does managing one bring?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q67) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q67) | [英](#q67)

   **A:** A virtual team consists of members distributed across different locations who collaborate through communication technology. Challenges include communication delays, time zone differences, cultural differences, difficulty in building trust, and lack of non-verbal cues. Countermeasures include establishing communication norms, using collaboration tools, increasing video meetings, and holding regular in-person gatherings.

<a id="q68"></a>
68. **Q: What does Situational Leadership theory refer to?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q68) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q68) | [英](#q68)

   **A:** Proposed by Hersey and Blanchard, the theory says the PM should adopt different leadership styles depending on subordinates' maturity: telling (low maturity), selling, participating, and delegating (high maturity). There is no single best style—only the most appropriate one—so the PM must be able to switch flexibly.

<a id="q69"></a>
69. **Q: How do you effectively motivate a team member whose performance has declined?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q69) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q69) | [英](#q69)

   **A:** First, understand the real reason through one-on-one conversations (family, burnout, task assignment); provide support based on the situation (training, task adjustment, mentoring); set clear goals and short-term wins; give timely feedback and recognition; when necessary, discuss career development directions to rebuild confidence and a sense of belonging.

<a id="q70"></a>
70. **Q: Please describe how you build a high-performing project team.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q70) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q70) | [英](#q70)

   **A:** Clarify the project vision and objectives; select complementary members based on a skills matrix; build a trust culture and create a team charter; use team-building activities to accelerate integration; assign clear roles (RACI); provide training and resource support; establish recognition mechanisms; continuously observe team dynamics and adapt as needed.

## VIII. Risk Management

<a id="q71"></a>
71. **Q: What are the main processes of project risk management?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q71) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q71) | [英](#q71)

   **A:** Six processes: Plan Risk Management, Identify Risks, Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis, Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis, Plan Risk Responses, Implement Risk Responses, and Monitor Risks. Risk management runs throughout the entire project life cycle and is a proactive—not reactive—process.

<a id="q72"></a>
72. **Q: What is the Risk Register?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q72) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q72) | [英](#q72)

   **A:** The Risk Register records all identified risks, including description, category, probability, impact, priority, owner, response strategy, and status. It is the core working document of risk management and must be continuously updated throughout the project and shared with the team.

<a id="q73"></a>
73. **Q: What are common risk identification methods?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q73) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q73) | [英](#q73)

   **A:** They include brainstorming, Delphi technique, interviews, SWOT analysis, checklists, assumption analysis, root cause analysis, fishbone diagrams, expert judgment, and reviews of historical projects. Using multiple methods together identifies risks more comprehensively, covering both known-knowns and known-unknowns.

<a id="q74"></a>
74. **Q: What is the difference between qualitative and quantitative risk analysis?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q74) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q74) | [英](#q74)

   **A:** Qualitative analysis prioritizes risks using a probability × impact matrix—fast and subjective; quantitative analysis uses numerical methods (e.g., Monte Carlo simulation, decision trees, EMV) to quantify risk impact on project objectives. Qualitative screens; quantitative performs in-depth analysis. Large projects often use both.

<a id="q75"></a>
75. **Q: What is a Probability-Impact Matrix?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q75) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q75) | [英](#q75)

   **A:** The Probability-Impact Matrix is a two-dimensional table with probability of occurrence on one axis and impact severity on the other, used to score and rank risks. Red/yellow/green zones distinguish priorities, helping the PM focus on high-priority risks. It is the most commonly used visualization tool in qualitative risk analysis.

<a id="q76"></a>
76. **Q: What are the four strategies for responding to negative risks (threats)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q76) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q76) | [英](#q76)

   **A:** Four strategies: Avoid (eliminate the source of the threat), Transfer (e.g., buy insurance or outsource), Mitigate (reduce probability or impact), and Accept (take no action or establish contingency reserves). Choice depends on risk priority and cost-benefit considerations.

<a id="q77"></a>
77. **Q: What are the four strategies for responding to positive risks (opportunities)?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q77) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q77) | [英](#q77)

   **A:** They are Exploit (ensure the opportunity is realized), Share (share the opportunity with partners), Enhance (increase probability or impact), and Accept (seize the opportunity as it comes). Opportunity management is equally important and can add value to the project.

<a id="q78"></a>
78. **Q: What are Secondary Risks and Residual Risks?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q78) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q78) | [英](#q78)

   **A:** Secondary risks are new risks that arise from implementing a risk response; residual risks are the risks remaining after responses have been applied. Both need to be recorded in the register and continuously monitored. Identifying secondary and residual risks reflects the PM's deep understanding of risk management.

<a id="q79"></a>
79. **Q: What is Monte Carlo Simulation?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q79) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q79) | [英](#q79)

   **A:** Monte Carlo simulation uses a large number of random samples and calculations to generate a probability distribution of project duration or cost, evaluating the likelihood of meeting objectives. Compared with single-point estimates, it quantifies uncertainty and provides a probabilistic perspective for decision-making. It is well suited for quantitative risk analysis on complex, large projects.

<a id="q80"></a>
80. **Q: Please describe a time you proactively managed risk on a project.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q80) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q80) | [英](#q80)

   **A:** On a system migration project, I identified early the risk of "critical supplier delivery delay." I developed a mitigation strategy (locking in the contract early, weekly progress tracking) and a contingency plan (an alternate supplier). When the supplier did indeed delay, the contingency plan was activated quickly, the project was delayed by only one week, and no critical milestones were affected.

## IX. Communications and Stakeholder Management

<a id="q81"></a>
81. **Q: Approximately how much time does a project manager spend on communication? Why?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q81) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q81) | [英](#q81)

   **A:** Studies show project managers spend about 90% of their time communicating. Because projects are inherently completed through collaboration between people, the PM needs to communicate continuously with stakeholders, the team, sponsors, suppliers, and others to ensure accurate information transfer, aligned expectations, and timely decisions. Communication is key to project success.

<a id="q82"></a>
82. **Q: What is the formula for the number of communication channels?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q82) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q82) | [英](#q82)

   **A:** Number of communication channels = n × (n − 1) / 2, where n is the number of stakeholders. For example, a 10-person team has 45 channels; 100 people have 4,950. Communication complexity grows exponentially with team size, so large projects need formal communication structures.

<a id="q83"></a>
83. **Q: What factors should be considered when developing a communications management plan?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q83) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q83) | [英](#q83)

   **A:** You must clarify: audience (who), content (what), method (meeting/email/report), frequency (when), sender (who is responsible), purpose, format, and confidentiality level. The plan should align with stakeholder engagement strategy to ensure information is delivered as needed.

<a id="q84"></a>
84. **Q: What are the three main communication methods?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q84) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q84) | [英](#q84)

   **A:** Interactive communication (meetings, phone—real-time, two-way), push communication (emails, reports—one-way distribution), and pull communication (knowledge bases, portals—recipient actively retrieves). Each has pros and cons; select based on urgency, complexity, and audience.

<a id="q85"></a>
85. **Q: What engagement levels are included in the Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q85) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q85) | [英](#q85)

   **A:** Five levels: Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, and Leading. The matrix records each key stakeholder's current (C) and desired (D) levels, and the PM should develop strategies to close the gap.

<a id="q86"></a>
86. **Q: What is the stakeholder Power/Interest Grid?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q86) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q86) | [英](#q86)

   **A:** A two-dimensional matrix, with interest (how much they care about the project) on one axis and power (their ability to influence the project) on the other. It has four quadrants: high power/high interest (manage closely), high power/low interest (keep satisfied), low power/high interest (keep informed), and low power/low interest (monitor). It guides differentiated management strategies.

<a id="q87"></a>
87. **Q: How do you manage a key stakeholder who opposes the project?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q87) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q87) | [英](#q87)

   **A:** First, deeply understand the reasons for opposition through one-on-one communication (conflict of interest, misunderstanding, historical issues); show empathy and look for common ground; use data and facts to counter misconceptions; invite them to participate in decisions to increase their sense of ownership; enlist their supervisor or allies for influence; and continuously follow up on the relationship.

<a id="q88"></a>
88. **Q: What are the key elements of running an effective project meeting?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q88) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q88) | [英](#q88)

   **A:** Clearly define the purpose and agenda and share them in advance; invite only necessary participants (screened via RACI); start and end on time; the facilitator controls the pace; encourage participation but avoid going off-topic; record decisions and action items (with owners and deadlines); and send minutes promptly after the meeting and follow up on execution.

<a id="q89"></a>
89. **Q: Please describe a time you handled a cross-cultural communication challenge.**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q89) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q89) | [英](#q89)

   **A:** I once led a China-Japan joint project. The Japanese side preferred written confirmations and details, while the Chinese side favored rapid iteration. I adjusted communication approaches: formal decisions were made through written documents, while daily stand-ups were retained. I trained both sides on cultural differences, built a bilingual glossary, and combined high-context and low-context expression. The project was delivered successfully.

<a id="q90"></a>
90. **Q: What core content should a project status report contain?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q90) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q90) | [英](#q90)

   **A:** It should include: overall project health (red/yellow/green), progress on key milestones, variances in scope/schedule/cost/quality and EVM metrics, major risks and issues, status of change requests, upcoming key actions and decision requests. The report should be concise and focused, highlighting the information stakeholders care about.

## X. Agile and Hybrid Methods

<a id="q91"></a>
91. **Q: What is Agile? What are its core values?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q91) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q91) | [英](#q91)

   **A:** Agile is an iterative and incremental project management approach originating from the 2001 Agile Manifesto. Its four core values are: individuals and interactions over processes and tools; working software over comprehensive documentation; customer collaboration over contract negotiation; responding to change over following a plan. It is suited to projects with uncertain requirements.

<a id="q92"></a>
92. **Q: What are the three roles in the Scrum framework?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q92) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q92) | [英](#q92)

   **A:** Product Owner (responsible for the product backlog and maximizing value), Scrum Master (a servant leader who helps the team follow Scrum and removes impediments), and Development Team (self-organizing and cross-functional, responsible for delivering a potentially shippable increment).

<a id="q93"></a>
93. **Q: What are the five Scrum events?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q93) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q93) | [英](#q93)

   **A:** Sprint (iteration, typically 1–4 weeks), Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum (daily stand-up), Sprint Review (demo the results of the sprint), and Sprint Retrospective (reflect and improve). The events form a closed loop that drives continuous inspection and adaptation.

<a id="q94"></a>
94. **Q: What are the three Scrum artifacts?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q94) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q94) | [英](#q94)

   **A:** Product Backlog (maintained by the PO), Sprint Backlog (maintained by the team), and Increment (the deliverable output produced in the current sprint). Each artifact has a corresponding commitment: Product Goal, Sprint Goal, and Definition of Done (DoD).

<a id="q95"></a>
95. **Q: What are the main differences between Scrum and Kanban?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q95) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q95) | [英](#q95)

   **A:** Scrum is based on time-boxed iterations, with fixed roles and events, and no work is added during a Sprint; Kanban is a continuous flow with no fixed iterations, emphasizing WIP (Work-in-Progress) limits and visualization, suited for ongoing tasks like operations. The two can be combined into Scrumban to leverage both strengths.

<a id="q96"></a>
96. **Q: What is a User Story? What does the INVEST principle stand for?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q96) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q96) | [英](#q96)

   **A:** A user story is a brief requirement described from the user's perspective: "As a... I want... so that...". INVEST stands for Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, and Testable.

<a id="q97"></a>
97. **Q: What are Story Points? Why not estimate in hours?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q97) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q97) | [英](#q97)

   **A:** Story points are a relative estimation unit that captures complexity, effort, and uncertainty combined. Relative estimation is more accurate, faster, and less affected by individual differences than absolute hours. Team velocity can be used to predict how many story points can be completed in future iterations.

<a id="q98"></a>
98. **Q: What is a Hybrid project management approach?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q98) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q98) | [英](#q98)

   **A:** Hybrid combines the strengths of predictive and adaptive approaches—for example, using waterfall (phases/milestones) for overall planning and agile (iterations) for detailed execution. It suits projects with some clear and some evolving requirements, such as enterprise system implementations where development uses agile and deployment uses waterfall.

<a id="q99"></a>
99. **Q: What is Servant Leadership?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q99) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q99) | [英](#q99)

   **A:** Servant leadership is the core style for agile PMs: the leader puts serving the team first, focusing on removing impediments, empowering the team, and helping members grow, rather than commanding and controlling. The Scrum Master is a typical servant leader. This style stimulates team autonomy and creativity.

<a id="q100"></a>
100. **Q: What challenges do you face when transitioning from traditional to agile project management, and how do you address them?**
   [中](02_pmp_interview.md#q100) | [日](02_pmp_interview_ja.md#q100) | [英](#q100)

   **A:** Challenges include: mindset shifts (from plan-driven to value-driven), organizational structure and process inertia, incompatible contract and budget models, management's desire for control, and team lack of agile skills. Response strategies: start with pilot projects, provide training and coaching, secure executive sponsorship, expand gradually, and establish an Agile Center of Excellence for continuous improvement.
