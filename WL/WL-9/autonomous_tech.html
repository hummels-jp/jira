Slide 3

Slide 3: Evolution of Autonomous Driving

What do you see in this image?

This slide illustrates the shift from AV 1.0 (based on sensor data and HD Maps with rule-based design) to AV 2.0 (centered on End-to-End AI), broken down into three major technological shifts:

  • Shift ① (2012~): CNN-based (Camera/LiDAR/RADAR)
  • Shift ② (2017~): CNN/Transformer-based (+ HDMap)
  • Shift ③ (2023~): Transformer/LLM-based (End-to-End AI)

Slide 4

Slide 4: AV 1.0 vs AV 2.0

What do you see in this image?

This slide compares AV 1.0 and AV 2.0 (E2E) systems:

  • AV 1.0: Rule-based systems, expensive high-cost sensors, low scalability, manual rule engineering, validation via on-road testing.
  • AV 2.0: Data-driven reactive simulators and E2E trainable stacks, affordable hardware, high scalability, learned behavior from human driving, validation via offline simulation.

Slide 5

Slide 5: System Types & Trade-offs

What do you see in this image?

This slide categorizes autonomous driving system types and illustrates their performance trade-offs along axes of interpretability, availability, and adaptability:

  • Robotics Approach (AV 1.0): High interpretability.
  • Perception AI + Planning AI (AV 2.0): Moderate performance.
  • E2E AI (AV 2.0): High availability and adaptability, but lower interpretability ("black-box").

The goal is to expand ML-based coverage to improve availability and reduce operational costs.

Slide 6

Slide 6: AV 2.0 Hybrid Architecture

What do you see in this image?

This slide introduces the AV 2.0 Hybrid Architecture, a framework that flexibly combines traditional robotics (AV 1.0) and E2E methods (AV 2.0):

  • Supports a wide range of approaches from robotics to E2E, allowing design and selection based on the deployment environment.
  • Coordinates E2E and robotics (rule-based) approaches to achieve both high performance and system safety.

Slide 7

Slide 7: AI Model Types

What do you see in this image?

This slide provides a comprehensive overview of AI models in autonomous driving, categorizing them into three primary types based on their architecture and functionality:

  • Perception AI (e.g., Mobileye): Uses AI for perception only, with human-written rules for decision/control. High interpretability/safety, low dev cost.
  • Perception AI + Planning AI (e.g., Waymo): AI for both perception and planning/prediction. Highly interpretable, allows rule-based safety integration.
  • End-to-End AI (e.g., Tesla / Wayve): Integrates sensing to control into a single model (Transformer-based or VLA). Optimizes performance but presents challenges for traditional safety validation due to its "black-box" nature.

Slide 8

Slide 8: Planning AI (Diffusion Planner)

What do you see in this image?

This slide introduces the Planning AI (Diffusion Planner), which uses Diffusion Transformer technology to rapidly infer vehicle trajectories based on route information, environmental perception, and map data:

  • Mechanism: Integrates data from Sensing, Map, Localization, Mission Planning, Perception, and Vehicle Status via an Encoder into a Diffusion Transformer.
  • Process: Performs denoising steps to generate stable trajectories for both moving objects and the ego-vehicle.
  • Performance: Achieving fast inference speeds of approximately 10-15 msec on an NVIDIA RTX4080.

Slide 9

Slide 9: Planning AI Learning Workflow

What do you see in this image?

This slide outlines the learning workflow for Planning AI, consisting of three progressive stages:

  • 1. Simulation + Real Data (Foundation): Initial acquisition of basic driving knowledge.
  • 2. High-Quality Small-Scale Real Data (SFT - Environment Adaptation): Fine-tuning for specific environments.
  • 3. Post-Training via Reinforcement Learning (Fine-tuning): Final micro-adjustments to optimize behavior.

Slide 12

Slide 12: End to End AI (BEVFusion + Diffusion Planner)

What do you see in this image?

This slide illustrates the comprehensive End to End AI pipeline integrating BEVFusion and Diffusion Planner:

  • Pipeline: Integrates data from Sensing, Map, Localization, Mission Planning, Vehicle Status, and BEVFusion features.
  • Core Components: These inputs are processed through an Encoder and fed into a Diffusion Transformer for trajectory generation.
  • Output: Generates unified trajectory predictions, including moving objects, ego-vehicle path, and turn signals, using a denoising process.

Slide 13

Slide 13: End to End AI (Alpamayo)

What do you see in this image?

This slide presents Alpamayo, an End-to-End AI approach that leverages large language models (LLMs) to perform complex reasoning tasks:

  • Functionality: Utilizes LLMs to generate high-level navigation instructions (e.g., "Keep lane to stay right of the traffic island").
  • Trajectory Inference: Concurrently infers driving trajectories based on these language-guided instructions, demonstrating a sophisticated integration of natural language understanding and motion planning in autonomous driving.

Slide 23

Slide 23: Value Scoring via Uncertainty & Reasoning

What do you see in this image?

This slide discusses how to implement Value Scoring through uncertainty estimation and reasoning:

  • Edge-Case Handling: Essential for addressing "unknown scenes" (e.g., bad weather, backlight, rare objects) where misjudgments can cause fatal accidents; efficiency in learning rare data is key.
  • Evidence-Based Improvement: Improves data collection and learning efficiency by verbalizing "why" a data point is considered high-uncertainty (reasoning).
